Choosing exterior color that survives Colorado sun.

Some colors hold for a decade here. Others are visibly tired in three. The difference is chemistry, not price.

Why color behaves differently at altitude

Three local conditions change how an exterior color reads and how long it lasts.

The light is harder. Thinner atmosphere scatters less, producing higher contrast and crisper shadows. Colors read lighter and more intense outdoors here than they do on a chip under store lighting. A gray that looked safe indoors can read almost white on a sunlit wall.

Reflected light changes by elevation. Near the foothills, west-facing walls catch warm bounce off rock and dry grass in late afternoon. East-facing walls get cool light off open sky in the morning. The same color genuinely reads warm on one wall and blue on another.

Snow rewrites everything for months. Fresh snow throws a large amount of cool reflected light onto lower walls. A scheme chosen in July can feel different in January.

Which pigments fade first

Fading is not random. It follows pigment chemistry, and the pattern is consistent.

Color familyDurability under UVWhy
Bright reds and orangesPoorestOrganic pigments break down fastest under ultraviolet
Bright yellowsPoorSame chemistry, highly visible when it shifts
Saturated blues and greensModerateMixed pigment systems, variable by formulation
Earth tones, tans, brownsGoodLargely inorganic iron oxide pigments
Grays and whitesBestHighly stable inorganic pigments

This is a large part of why Front Range exteriors trend toward earth tones and grays. It is not only taste. Those schemes still look intentional after eight summers.

If you want a saturated accent, put it somewhere protected. A recessed entry door under a porch roof holds a deep red far longer than a south-facing garage door will.

Sampling properly

Almost everyone samples badly, and then blames the color.

  1. Paint boards, not the house. Two coats on a two-foot square of poster board, so you can move it.
  2. Put one on every elevation. North, south, east and west genuinely differ here.
  3. Look at three times of day. Morning, mid-afternoon and dusk.
  4. Leave them up for several days. First impressions of color are unreliable.
  5. Hold them against what is not changing: roof, stone, brick, window frames. Those elements are fixed and they outvote your preference.

Working inside an HOA palette

Covenant communities publish approved colors, and homeowners often treat that as a creative dead end. It usually is not.

  • Most palettes approve body, trim and accent separately, and the combination is where the character comes from.
  • The same approved body color reads very differently with a near-black trim than with a cream.
  • Front doors are frequently excluded from the palette or governed loosely. That is your saturated color, and it is the element people actually notice.
  • Submit early. Boards meet on a schedule, and a late submission delays the crew, not the board.

Three combinations that hold up

These are durable pigment families, not fashion picks.

  • Warm greige body, bronze trim, deep red door. Earth tones throughout, with the one vulnerable color protected in a recess.
  • Mid-gray body, near-black trim, natural wood door. Both body and trim sit in the most UV-stable families.
  • Sandstone body, cream trim, muted green accent. Reads as the local landscape and ages gradually rather than abruptly.

One practical note

Whatever you choose, record the product, color code and sheen for each surface, or ask your painter to. Two years later when a downspout bracket gets replaced and needs touching up, that record is worth considerably more than the half-used can in the garage.

Questions

Common questions

What exterior colors last longest in Colorado?

Earth tones, tans, grays and whites, because they rely on inorganic pigments that resist ultraviolet breakdown. Bright reds, oranges and yellows fade fastest.

Why does my color look different on the house than the chip?

Altitude and light. Thinner atmosphere means harder, less scattered light, so colors read lighter and more intense outdoors. Sample on full-size boards on each elevation instead.

How should I sample exterior colors?

Two coats on movable poster boards, one on each elevation, viewed morning, afternoon and dusk over several days, held against fixed elements like roof and stone.

Can I have an accent color in an HOA community?

Often yes. Most palettes approve body, trim and accent separately, and front doors are frequently excluded or loosely governed. The combination is where character comes from.
