Color, Lakewood light

Choosing paint color in Lakewood light.

Bright, high-altitude sun washes colors out outdoors and makes north rooms feel cool indoors. Sample before you commit.

Free estimateCall 720-208-5645
In one breath

To choose paint colors for a Lakewood home, sample large swatches on several walls and view them morning and afternoon. Exterior colors look lighter in strong Colorado sun, so go a shade deeper than the chip; use light reflectance value (LRV) to brighten dim rooms; and check HOA rules before changing exterior colors.

Five rules that save repaints

  1. Never choose from a 2-inch chip. Paint or stick up large samples.
  2. Look at samples at different times of day and under your own bulbs.
  3. Outdoors, bright sun lightens colors; go a step deeper than you think.
  4. Indoors, north-facing rooms read cooler; warmer whites help.
  5. Check your HOA's approved palette before an exterior change.

What LRV tells you

Light reflectance value, printed on most chips, runs from 0 (black) to 100 (pure white). Higher numbers bounce more light, useful for basements and halls. Very high LRV on a sunny exterior can glare.

Color for exteriors near the foothills

Earthy greens, warm grays and stone tones sit comfortably against the hogback. Very dark bodies absorb more heat in direct sun; trims and doors are the safest place for bold color.

Straight answers

Should exterior colors be darker or lighter than the chip?

Strong Colorado sun makes colors read lighter outdoors, so many people choose one step deeper than the chip they like.

What is LRV in paint?

Light reflectance value, from 0 to 100, measures how much light a color reflects. Higher LRV brightens dim rooms.

Which paint brand is best?

It depends on the surface. J.D. Power's 2026 study ranked Benjamin Moore top for interior and Behr top for exterior paint; we use all major brands.
