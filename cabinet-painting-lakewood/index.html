Bighorn Painting paints and refinishes kitchen cabinets across Lakewood, Colorado. Cabinets are degreased, scuff-sanded, primed with a bonding primer formulated for slick factory finishes, and sprayed with a cabinet-grade topcoat. Four steps, and skipping one causes every chip

Nearly every failed cabinet job we are asked to fix skipped at least one of these.

  1. Degrease. Cabinets near a cooktop carry years of aerosolized cooking residue. No primer bonds through grease.
  2. Scuff sand. Factory finishes are deliberately slick so food wipes off. That same slickness rejects coatings. Sanding creates tooth.
  3. Bonding primer. Not ordinary primer. A product formulated specifically to grip a cured factory finish.
  4. Cabinet-grade topcoat. Wall paint never cures hard enough to survive fingernails and doors closing a hundred times a week.

Why sprayed matters

You can identify a brushed cabinet door from across a room. Brush marks, paint pooling at panel edges, a surface that never reads as furniture. Spraying lays the coating down flat and even, which is the entire point of refinishing instead of replacing.

Doors and drawer fronts come off, get labeled, and are sprayed in a controlled area. Boxes stay in your kitchen and are sprayed in place behind containment, so every surface matches.

The countertop detail people miss

Replacing cabinets almost always drags countertop replacement with it, because old counters rarely survive removal and almost never fit new boxes. That can add several thousand dollars that a replacement quote may not make obvious. Refinishing leaves your counters exactly where they are.

What the week looks like

Day one: doors and drawer fronts removed, labeled, hardware bagged and tracked. Days one and two: boxes degreased, sanded and masked behind containment. Days two and three: bonding primer, with sanding between coats. Days three and four: finish coats. Day five: doors rehung, hardware reinstalled, hinges adjusted, walkthrough. Cure time between coats sets the schedule, not labor.

Questions

Cabinet painting questions

How much does cabinet painting cost in Lakewood?

Front Range figures for a professionally refinished kitchen generally land between $2,000 and $8,000. Three things set where you fall: how many doors and drawer fronts there are, whether the carcasses are included, and what shape the existing finish is in.

Is refinishing really cheaper than new cabinets?

Substantially. Replacement commonly starts near $15,000 and runs past $30,000 for custom work, and it usually brings countertop replacement with it. Refinishing leaves your counters in place.

Do you spray or brush?

Sprayed, always. Fronts come off and are finished in a controlled space; the carcasses are sprayed where they stand behind sheeting. A brushed door announces itself from the far side of the room.

How long is my kitchen out of action?

Three to five days on site. You keep countertops and appliances throughout. Doors are off for most of that, so plan on open shelving for the week.

Will the finish chip?

Not when the process is right. Chipping traces back to skipped degreasing, no scuff sanding, missing bonding primer, or wall paint used where a cabinet-grade coating belongs.

Can you fix a cabinet job someone else did badly?

Usually. We assess whether the existing coating can be sanded and recoated or has to come back to bare, then rebuild the finish properly.
